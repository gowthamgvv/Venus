CIBIL Awareness & Financial Education

Know your score.
Own your future.

Your credit score is a three-digit number that tells every bank and NBFC how reliably you repay. It decides whether a loan is approved, how much you get and what interest you pay. Understanding it early saves lakhs over a lifetime.

The one thing to remember

Too many loan enquiries pull your score down.

Every time you apply for a loan or credit card, the lender checks your credit report. This is called a hard enquiry and it is recorded on your report. One or two are normal. Many in a short time tell lenders you are desperate for credit, so your score drops and approvals get harder.

1You apply at many placesBanks, NBFCs, loan apps and showroom finance desks, all in a few days.
2Each one is a hard enquiryEvery lender pulls your report and the enquiry is logged against your name.
3Your score slipsEach enquiry can lower the score by a few points, and several together add up.
4Rejections followThe next lender sees a lower score and many enquiries, and may decline or raise the rate.
Do this
  • ✔ Check your own score first. Checking your own report is a soft enquiry and does not lower it.
  • ✔ Ask us for eligibility guidance before you apply anywhere.
  • ✔ Apply to one or two well-matched lenders, not ten.
  • ✔ Keep documents complete so the first application is not rejected.
  • ✔ If a loan is declined, find out why and wait a few months before trying again.
Avoid this
  • ✘ Applying at every bank and app to see who says yes.
  • ✘ Tapping "check instant approval" links and loan apps without reading what they pull.
  • ✘ Re-applying the same week after a rejection.
  • ✘ Applying for a new card or loan just before a home or car loan.
  • ✘ Letting a dealer or agent send your documents to many lenders at once.

Good news: the effect of an enquiry fades over time, and a steady record of on-time payments helps your score recover. Enquiries stay visible on your report for a couple of years, so it pays to apply carefully from the start.

What is a CIBIL score?

CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL) is one of India's credit information companies. It collects your loan and credit card repayment records from banks and NBFCs and turns them into a score from 300 to 900, with a detailed credit report behind it. Other bureaus such as Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark keep similar records and scores.

Lenders look at the score and the report together: how many loans you hold, whether you paid on time and how much of your limit you use.

Score bands at a glance

300550650700750900

Below 550 · Poor. Approval is very difficult. Fix overdues first.

550–649 · Fair. Limited offers, higher interest.

650–699 · Average. Approval possible, rates not the best.

700–749 · Good. Most lenders will consider you.

750 and above · Excellent. Faster approvals and better rate negotiation.

Bands are a general guide. Each lender sets its own cut-off and policy.

What moves your score

Five habits, one number.

The weights below are commonly cited approximations. The exact formula is not published by the bureau.

Payment historyabout 35%

Every EMI and card bill paid on or before the due date builds your score. One missed payment can stay on your report for years.

Credit utilisationabout 30%

Try to use less than 30% of your card limit. Using 90% of a ₹1,00,000 limit every month signals stress, even if you pay it back.

Length of credit historyabout 15%

Older accounts in good standing help. Avoid closing your oldest credit card without a reason.

Credit mixabout 10%

A healthy balance of secured loans (home, vehicle) and unsecured credit (cards, personal loans) shows you can manage both.

New enquiriesabout 10%

Each loan or card application triggers a hard enquiry. Many applications in a short time make lenders cautious. Compare through one adviser instead of applying everywhere.

Myths vs facts

Clear the confusion.

Myth: Checking my own score lowers it.

Fact: Checking your own report is a soft enquiry and does not reduce your score.

Myth: I have no loans, so my score must be high.

Fact: With no credit history there is no score at all. Lenders need some repayment record to rate you.

Myth: Paying only the minimum due on my card is fine.

Fact: The minimum keeps the account current, but the balance carries heavy interest and keeps utilisation high.

Myth: Closing a loan with a settlement clears the record.

Fact: A "settled" status shows you paid less than owed and can hurt future approvals. "Closed" after full payment is what you want.

Myth: Being a guarantor or co-applicant carries no risk.

Fact: If the borrower defaults, it shows on your report too.

Take action

Check it. Fix it. Grow it.

Under RBI rules you can get one free full credit report every year from each credit bureau. Check it at least once a year, and before any big loan.

  1. 1Get your reportDownload it from the bureau's official site or app using your PAN and mobile number.
  2. 2Read every accountLook for loans you never took, wrong overdue marks, or closed accounts shown as open.
  3. 3Raise a disputeReport errors to the bureau and the lender. Keep proof such as the closure letter or payment receipt.
  4. 4Build a steady habitAuto-debit EMIs, keep card use low, and wait a few months between applications.
